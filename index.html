Our Story

Durban's Hidden Gem

"A walk through Mitchell Park is a walk through the soul of Durban."

Nestled in the leafy suburb of Morningside, Mitchell Park Zoo has captivated visitors since 1898. This beloved municipal zoo — managed by eThekwini Municipality — is one of Durban's most treasured landmarks: a lush escape where children press their faces to glass to watch chameleons, families picnic beneath century-old trees, and peacocks strut freely through manicured gardens.

Spanning approximately 6 hectares, the park blends a traditional zoological collection with stunning botanical displays, making it a uniquely comprehensive destination for wildlife and plant lovers alike.

  • 50+ animal species from across Africa and beyond
  • Spectacular tropical and indigenous plant collections
  • Over 120 years of cherished community heritage
  • Picnic lawns, shaded paths & children's play areas
  • Open daily — perfect for spontaneous family days out

Pavo cristatus · Indian Peacock

The Stars of the Park

No visit to Mitchell Park Zoo is complete without encountering one of its free-roaming peacocks. These magnificent birds wander the garden paths with regal confidence, often spreading their spectacular tail feathers — called a "train" — in a dazzling courtship display that stops every visitor mid-step.

The train can reach up to 1.8 metres in length and contains over 200 feathers, each decorated with an iridescent "eye" that shimmers between blue, green and gold. The male fans his train and vibrates the feathers to create a hypnotic rustling sound.

Chlorocebus pygerythrus · Vervet Monkey

Africa's Most Expressive Primate

Vervet monkeys have an elaborate alarm-call system with distinct calls for different predators — aerial threats, land predators and snakes each trigger a different call, and troop members respond appropriately without even seeing the threat.

Mitchell Park's vervets live in a spacious enclosure where visitors observe their rich social lives — grooming sessions, infant care, play behaviour and territorial displays all unfold naturally throughout the day.

Stigmochelys pardalis · Leopard Tortoise

A Living Fossil

The leopard tortoise is Africa's gentle giant — the continent's largest tortoise and one that has barely changed in millions of years. Their domed shells, patterned like a leopard's coat, are as unique as fingerprints: no two are identical.

These remarkable animals can live over a century, store water in their bladder for dry seasons, and travel surprising distances in search of food. At Mitchell Park, they move slowly through their sunny enclosure — a living connection to prehistoric Africa.

Botanical Gardens

A Garden Paradise

Mitchell Park is as much a botanical sanctuary as it is a zoo. Walking the shaded paths, you'll encounter ancient cycads that outlived the dinosaurs, explosions of bougainvillea colour, the iconic Bird of Paradise in full bloom, and tranquil ponds draped in water lilies.

  • Bird of Paradise (Strelitzia reginae) — SA's iconic bloom
  • King Protea — South Africa's national flower
  • Ancient cycads — living fossils, 250M years old
  • Koi ponds with water lilies & papyrus
  • Bougainvillea, hibiscus, agapanthus & aloes
  • Seasonal displays bloom year-round in Durban's climate

Featured Plants & Flowers

From South Africa's national flower to ancient living fossils — every plant in the park tells a story.

SA Icon
Strelitzia reginae

Bird of Paradise

South Africa's most celebrated flower — vivid orange and electric blue petals resembling an exotic bird in flight. Named after Queen Charlotte of Mecklenburg-Strelitz, this is the official flower of Los Angeles and grows abundantly throughout the park.

King Protea

South Africa's national flower — a stunning architectural bloom up to 30cm across. The king protea belongs to one of the world's oldest plant families, with fossil records dating back 300 million years. Its lineage connects to a time before Africa and South America separated.

Agapanthus (Blue Lily)

Known as the "Lily of the Nile" — spherical clusters of blue-violet trumpet flowers. In Zulu culture, this plant holds deep spiritual significance, used in protective medicine and connected to fertility and childbirth across generations.

Tips

Visitor Tips

🌅
Arrive Early
Morning visits (08:30–11:00) are best — animals are most active and feeding. You'll see chameleons hunting, monkeys playing and birds at peak energy.
☀️
Sun Protection
Durban's subtropical sun is strong year-round. Hats, sunscreen and sunglasses are essential, especially in summer months (Nov–Mar).
🧺
Pack a Picnic
Mitchell Park has beautiful shaded picnic lawns. Bring a blanket and a basket — lunch beneath a century-old tree with peacocks strutting past is unforgettable.
📷
Bring Your Camera
The zoo and gardens offer extraordinary photography opportunities. Morning light is exceptional. A long lens helps for bird photography; wide-angle captures the garden grandeur.
👟
Comfortable Shoes
The park has winding paths and gentle slopes. Flat, closed shoes are recommended — especially if bringing young children who'll want to explore everywhere.

FAQs

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the zoo open on public holidays?

+
Yes! Mitchell Park Zoo is open every day of the year, including all South African public holidays. Hours remain 08:30–17:00. The park is particularly popular on holidays — arrive early to avoid queues.

Is there parking available?

+
Yes, free parking is available at the main entrance on Mitchell Park Road. It can be limited on busy weekends, so arrive early. Additional street parking is available in the surrounding Morningside suburb.

Is the park wheelchair and pram accessible?

+
The park has paved walking paths throughout, making it largely accessible for prams and wheelchairs. Some garden areas have uneven terrain. Toilet facilities are available at the park.

Can I bring a picnic?

+
Absolutely — and we encourage it! Mitchell Park has beautiful grassed picnic areas with shaded tables. Pack a picnic basket and enjoy lunch surrounded by the sights and sounds of the zoo. Please use the bins to keep the park clean.

Are there facilities for children?

+
Yes! The park has a children's play area, toilet facilities and baby-changing facilities. The grounds are safe and child-friendly, with most enclosures designed for easy viewing even by small children.

How long should I plan for a visit?

+
Most visitors spend 2–3 hours at the park, but a full leisurely day including a picnic and exploring every enclosure is entirely possible. We recommend at least 2 hours to do the park justice.

Are pets allowed?

+
Pet dogs are not permitted inside the park as they can disturb the animals. Service animals with the appropriate documentation are welcome.

✅ What to Bring

  • Sunscreen & hat — Durban sun is strong
  • Comfortable walking shoes
  • Camera for incredible photo opportunities
  • Picnic blanket & lunch for the lawns
  • Water bottle — stay hydrated
  • Cash for entry fees
  • Binoculars for birdwatching

📋 Park Rules

  • 👶Supervise children at all times near enclosures
